YOUR BEST TIME TO CONSIDER THE CONTROLS IS WHILE PLANNING THE SPACE
THE SHORT ANSWER

Think about automation before electrical decisions are locked.

The useful planning window is usually when your interior designer and electrician are deciding switch locations, circuits and appliance points. You do not need a complete smart-home installation at that stage. You do need a realistic list of desired functions, awareness of device compatibility and a plan for who will install and support them.

If your interior work is already finished, a retrofit may still be possible. Feasibility depends on existing wiring, switchboard space, power requirements, wireless coverage and the specific devices selected.

WHEN TO DECIDE WHAT

Three checkpoints for a better move-in.

STAGE 01 / LAYOUT PLANNING

Decide what you want to control

Walk through a normal day in your future home. Which lights and fans do you use most? Would an arrival, bedtime or away routine be useful? Should some devices be controlled from a phone, wall switch or both? Share the answers with your interior designer before layouts are fixed.

STAGE 02 / ELECTRICAL COORDINATION

Check hardware requirements

Ask a qualified electrical professional to verify circuit loads, switchboard dimensions, neutral-wire requirements where relevant, appliance suitability, network connectivity and the selected product's specifications. Different devices have different requirements, so avoid assuming everything is interchangeable.

STAGE 03 / COMMISSIONING & HANDOVER

Test the way you'll actually live

Confirm that every agreed switch or routine works, understand what happens if internet connectivity is unavailable, learn the controls, check access permissions and request written warranty/support details. Record any unfinished items before final handover.

ROOM-BY-ROOM PLANNING

What is worth discussing early?

These are examples to help structure a consultation, not promises that every device is compatible with every property.

LIVING & DINING

Light, ambience and familiar controls

Identify the main lighting circuits, fans and the scenes you might want for arriving, entertaining or watching television. Decide where physical switches remain essential.

BEDROOMS

Simple routines and comfort

Think about bedside switch access, appropriate fan controls, night-time lighting and timed actions on compatible equipment. Avoid complex automations that make manual control harder.

KITCHEN & UTILITY

Convenience with electrical safety

List appliances you actually want to schedule or monitor. Ask a qualified electrician to assess appliance ratings and approved device compatibility; do not put high-load appliances on an unsuitable smart plug.

ENTRANCE & SAFETY

Access and awareness

Explore options for supported locks, video doorbells, occupancy sensing or gas/smoke alerts. Discuss installation, maintenance and what each device can—and cannot—detect or control.

Bring this list to your interior designer and installer

  • Floor plan with room and switch locations
  • List of lighting circuits, fans and appliances
  • Move-in date and current electrical/interior stage
  • Internet/router locations and expected wireless coverage
  • Desired wall control, phone control or voice-assistant features
  • Realistic budget range and which rooms matter most
  • Expectations for installation, training, warranty and after-sales support
BEFORE YOU BUY

Four avoidable planning mistakes.

01

Buying switches before checking wiring

Some smart devices need particular wiring, a neutral connection or enough depth behind a switchboard. Confirm product requirements and site conditions first.

02

Confusing app features with complete integration

Several products may each have an app but may not work together. Ask which combinations and routines are actually supported in the proposed system.

03

Ignoring manual controls and handover

A well-designed home should still be easy for family members and visitors to operate. Ask how the controls work offline and how user access will be managed.

04

Comparing quotations without matching the scope

Two prices can include different switch counts, hubs, installation work, programming, warranty terms or taxes. Ask for an itemized scope before judging the price.

QUESTIONS WE EXPECT DURING INTERIOR PLANNING

Know before you commit.

Is smart home automation only for new flats?

No. Existing homes may have retrofit options. Product selection will depend on electrical conditions, compatibility and whether additional work is needed.

Do I need to automate every room?

No. Prioritize the few controls and routines that you will use frequently. Compatible functions can often be expanded later after checking the system design.

Can my electrician install any smart switch?

Not automatically. Electrical work should follow the selected product's approved instructions and relevant safety requirements. Ask the manufacturer or installation partner to confirm compatibility and qualifications.

Does planning early mean paying for everything now?

Not necessarily. It can mean making informed design and wiring decisions before choosing the full final scope. Your installer should explain any prerequisites and phased options.
